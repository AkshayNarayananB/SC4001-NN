# ViewLayer: blurred/flipped views read out at a middle layer (our method)

## Why

Two findings from our own Phase 2 runs (TEST images, corrected AUROC):

- **More views help.** In the RITUAL runs, averaging all six transformed views gave the most consistent
  AUROC gain of any method (above baseline in 15/27 cells). In the per-transform ablation, a
  **slightly blurred copy of a noisy image** helped most: noise s4 AUROC went from 0.737 to 0.751.
  A small blur acts as a free denoiser.
- **Middle layers know more.** In the DeCo runs, layer 20 *on its own* separates present from absent
  objects better than the final layer, and the gap grows with degradation:
  +0.003 on clean images, **+0.029 at blur s4**, +0.017 at noise s4. DeCo's mixing weight (α·p_max)
  is too small to use this.

Neither paper combines the two, so this method does. Like RITUAL and DeCo, it needs no training and
no second "blind" pass.

## Method

For each question:
1. Run LLaVA once, batched, on the degraded image and a few views of it: Gaussian blur σ = 1, 2 or 3 px, and a horizontal flip.
2. Read every layer's Yes − No logit through the final norm and the LM head, as in DeCo and DoLa.
3. Score = **mean over a view set V of the Yes−No margin at layer L**. Answer "yes" if score > t.

**Leakage-free selection.**
- V, L and t are all chosen on the **100 DEV images only**, one global choice across every condition. The method never needs to know how degraded an image is.
- V and L maximise mean DEV AUROC. t maximises mean DEV F1.
- Since t is tuned, the baseline also gets its own DEV-tuned threshold in the F1 comparison.
- All reported numbers are on the **400 TEST images**.

**Ablations** (also selected on DEV):
- **layer-only:** original image, best layer. This is DeCo's signal alone.
- **views-only:** best views, final layer. This is RITUAL's signal alone.

**How it differs from the pasted ideas and Fix B.**
- **#1 (zeroed visual KV-cache) and #2 (shuffled positional encodings):** nothing is blinded or subtracted here.
- **Fix B (DAE):** no trained restoration network. The blur view is a fixed filter added alongside the original image, not a replacement for it.

```bash
python -m pytest ViewLayer/tests -q                     # local CPU tests
```

Raw records: `$VCD_OUTPUT_ROOT/viewlayer_<kind>_s<level>/<split>.jsonl` (32 layer margins × 5 views per question).

## Caveats

- The *idea* of a blur view came from looking at TEST-image results in the RITUAL ablation. The
  *choices* (which views, which layer, threshold) are made on DEV only, but the candidate list was
  informed by earlier results. We state this openly in the report.
- The blur view is expected to help noise more than blur, because blurring an already-blurred image adds nothing.
